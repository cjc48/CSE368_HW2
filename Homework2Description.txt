1 Background
A language model assigns a score, called a logit, to each possible next token. Logits are converted into probabilities using the softmax function. For logits z_1, z_2, ..., z_n the probability of selecting token i is:

P(i) = e^(z_i) / (sum_{j=1}^n e^(z_j))

The resulting probabilities lie between 0 and 1 and sum to 1.

1.1 Greedy Decoding
Greedy decoding selects the token with the highest logit, which is also the token with the highest softmax probability:

i* = arg max_i z_i

When the logits remain unchanged, greedy decoding always selects the same token.

1.2 Random Sampling
Random sampling selects a token according to the probability distribution produced by softmax. A token with a higher probability is more likely to be selected, but tokens with lower probabilities may also be selected.

Random sampling is not the same as uniformly selecting a random token. Selection probabilities must be based on the model's logits.

1.3 Temperature-Based Sampling
Temperature-based sampling adjusts the logits before applying softmax. The temperature parameter T controls how concentrated or spread out the probability distribution becomes:

P(i|T) = e^(z_i / T) / (sum_{j=1}^n e^(z_j / T))

The effects of temperature are:
- T < 1: The distribution generally becomes more concentrated around the highest-scoring tokens.
- T = 1: The distribution is the same as ordinary softmax sampling.
- T > 1: The distribution becomes flatter, making lower-scoring tokens relatively more likely.

Temperature must be greater than zero.
